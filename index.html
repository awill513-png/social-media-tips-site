Analytics 101

The metrics that actually matter

Your insights tab shows dozens of numbers. These six are worth checking every week.

Reach

How many different accounts saw your post. This is how far your content travels.

Watch: reach from non-followers. That's how new people find you.

Engagement rate

Likes + comments + shares + saves, divided by reach. The best single measure of how much people care about a post.

Benchmark: 1–3% is average, 3–6% is strong, and 6% or more is excellent.

Saves & shares

The strongest signals to the algorithm. They mean "worth coming back to" or "my friends need this."

Watch: which formats get saved most. Make more of those.

Watch time

For video: average view duration and the percent who watch to the end.

Watch: the drop-off point on your retention graph.

Follower growth rate

New followers ÷ starting followers over a set period. More useful than your total follower count.

Watch: spikes. Find the post that caused them.

Clicks & conversions

Link taps, sign-ups and sales that came from social. Track them with UTM links.

Watch: which platform drives action, not just likes.

The big picture

How one post turns into results

Every metric fits somewhere in this funnel. When results are weak, find the stage where people drop off and fix that first.

  • Low reach? Work on hooks, timing and trending formats.
  • Reach is fine but engagement is low? Your content isn't delivering on its hook.
  • Engagement is fine but clicks are low? Make the call-to-action clearer.

Your weekly routine

Read your analytics in 15 minutes

1

Pick one goal per platform

Awareness (reach), community (engagement), or action (clicks). Judge each platform by its own goal.

2

Find your top 3 and bottom 3 posts

Sort by engagement rate, not likes. Note what the winners share: format, topic, hook, length or time.

3

Compare to your own average

A post doing twice your usual numbers is a hit, even if the numbers look small next to big creators.

4

Check where viewers came from

Feed, Explore/For You, hashtags, search or your profile. This shows which discovery channel to focus on.

5

Run one experiment

Change only one thing next week (hook, time, format or caption length) and compare.

💡
Pro trick: Keep a simple spreadsheet with these columns: Date · Platform · Format · Topic · Hook · Reach · Eng. rate · Saves · Notes. After a month, patterns jump out.

Timing

When should you post?

Common starting points are weekday mornings, lunch, and evenings after 6 PM. Your audience is unique, so check the "Most active times" section of your insights and test from there.

  • Post 30–60 minutes before your audience peaks.
  • Stay online for 15–30 minutes after posting to reply.
  • Being consistent matters more than finding the perfect minute.

Works everywhere

Universal posting tricks

🪝 Hook first

Open with a question, a surprising number, a bold claim, or the end result.

♻️ Repurpose

One idea can become a Reel, a TikTok, a carousel and a Story series.

📣 Clear CTA

"Save this for later." "Send to a friend who…" Tell people exactly what to do.

📅 Consistency

Posting 3 times a week, every week, beats 10 posts one week and nothing for a month.

🧪 80/20 rule

Make 80% of posts proven formats and 20% experiments. Your analytics tell you which is which.

🔗 UTM links

Tag links with ?utm_source=tiktok so you can see which platform drives traffic.
